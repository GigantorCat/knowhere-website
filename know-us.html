a note from the founder

School and I did not get along.

Not because I was causing trouble — I wasn't. I just wasn't built for the format. My brain moved fast and sideways: I'd get a topic in minutes and then sit through an hour of a teacher reading a textbook at me about something I already understood. I took notes in pictures because that's how my brain files things. For all of this, I was treated as the problem.

I spent most of Year 12 in the school library, because removing me from class was easier for everyone than teaching a brain like mine. I passed the time photocopying my face for a self-portrait nobody had asked for. Then I sat the exams and finished with the top score in my school and the top percentile in the state — mostly without attending class.

I'm not telling you that to brag. I'm telling you because it's evidence. The gap was never ability. It was format. The system decided what a "good student" looked like, and if your brain worked differently, you were graded on the difference instead of the intelligence.

That was twenty years ago. It hasn't improved. The kid drawing pictures in the margins is still being told to stop.

So I stopped waiting for the system to fix itself. I built the thing I needed.

— Cat, founder
