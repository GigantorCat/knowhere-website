When only one driver pushes for land use change — say, rising land values in isolation — the conversion often stalls. Landowners face uncertainty, planners resist without broader justification, and communities mobilise opposition. Single-factor pressure rarely overcomes the inertia of existing land use, even when that factor is strong. This is why economic booms don't automatically trigger mass development, and why policy changes alone rarely reshape landscapes without accompanying social or technological shifts.
Know This
Land use change almost always results from several drivers acting together — economic pressure (land value, development demand), social drivers (population growth, changing needs), and technological and political forces combine to overcome the inertia of existing land use patterns.
