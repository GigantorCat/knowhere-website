Amino acids, charge and pKa: an amino acid whose ionisable groups gain and lose protons as the pH changes, beside its net charge curve and titration curve

At zero net charge, an amino acid still carries two charges.

Each ionisable group on an amino acid holds its proton below its pKa and lets it go above it, so the net charge is a sum of fractions that falls as the pH rises. Raise the pH from strong acid to strong base and watch each group give up its proton, the net charge pass through zero, and where that zero lands for each amino acid.

Know this
