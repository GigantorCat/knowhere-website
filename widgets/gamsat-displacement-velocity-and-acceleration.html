Distance isn't displacement: a round trip has zero average velocity

If you drive around the block and return to your starting point, you've travelled hundreds of metres but your displacement is exactly zero. So your average velocity for the trip is zero too, even though you were never at rest and your speedometer never read zero. Displacement is a vector quantity measured in metres with a specific direction from start to finish. It's the straight-line distance between where you started and where you ended up, regardless of the path you took. If you walk 3 metres north then 4 metres east, your total distance is 7 metres but your displacement is 5 metres at a specific angle. When you return to your starting position, no matter how far you've travelled, your displacement is zero because the vector from start to finish has zero magnitude.

Know This
Displacement is a vector from start to finish, velocity is its rate of change per second, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity per second squared.
