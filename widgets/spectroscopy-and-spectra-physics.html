Heat hydrogen gas and it glows at sharp wavelengths — 656 nm red, 486 nm cyan, 434 nm violet. Each line is one electron dropping from a higher level to n=2 (the Balmer series). The photon's energy equals the gap: E = hf, so bigger jumps make bluer light. Every element has a unique set of allowed levels, so every element has a unique spectrum — astronomy's greatest forensic tool.
Know This

Atomic spectra are discrete lines because electrons occupy quantised energy levels, and each photon released carries exactly the energy difference between two levels.
