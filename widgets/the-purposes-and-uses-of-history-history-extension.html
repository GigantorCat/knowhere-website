The past is not neutral — it always serves

History is not a passive record. The same event can be memorial to one group, a lesson to another, identity to a third, and a weapon to a fourth. Use the slider to see how each purpose reshapes what the past "says."

Every telling of history is a choice about what matters. When the same event serves as memorial, it centres grief and remembrance — names, dates, loss. As lesson, it abstracts patterns and warnings for the present. As identity, it defines who "we" are and anchors collective belonging. As weapon, it justifies current claims, delegitimises opponents, or mobilises action. Extension History asks you to recognise these purposes in motion — not to dismiss them, but to see that no account is innocent of purpose. The question is never whether history serves a purpose, only which purpose it serves and who benefits.
