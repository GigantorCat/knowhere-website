When the reaction runs forward, reactants A and B are consumed and product C is formed. The change row shows negative values for A and B (they decrease) and positive for C (it increases). The stoichiometric coefficients — 2:1:3 — determine the exact ratio of these changes. If 0.40 M of A is consumed, then 0.20 M of B must also be consumed (half as much, because the coefficient of B is 1 while that of A is 2), and 0.60 M of C is produced (1.5 times the A change, because the coefficient of C is 3 while that of A is 2). This ratio is locked by the balanced equation and never changes, regardless of direction or how far the reaction proceeds.

Know This

The change row of an ICE table obeys the stoichiometric ratio of the balanced equation — one known change determines all others through simple proportion, and row 3 equals row 1 plus row 2 to give equilibrium concentrations you substitute into K.
