Feudalism and legal privilege were genuinely abolished—the seigneurial system, tithes, and noble exemptions vanished permanently. Equality before the law became enshrined in new legal codes. The metric system, civil marriage, and centralised administration replaced centuries of regional chaos. These were structural transformations, not rhetoric. By 1795 France had a republic, a professional bureaucracy, and a society where birth no longer guaranteed power. The revolution demolished the ancien régime's legal architecture and never rebuilt it.

Know This

The Revolution was structurally radical but socially incomplete—it destroyed feudalism and legal privilege while leaving economic inequality and gendered exclusion untouched.
