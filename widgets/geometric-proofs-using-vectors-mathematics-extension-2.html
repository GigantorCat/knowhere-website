Given triangle ABC with position vectors a, b, c, the medians meet at the centroid G = (a + b + c)/3. To prove: show that G lies on each median. The midpoint of BC is m = (b + c)/2. The median from A is the line r = a + λ(m − a). Substitute G: we need (a + b + c)/3 = a + λ((b + c)/2 − a). Solving gives λ = 2/3, confirming G lies on this median. The other two medians follow by symmetry. No diagram needed — the algebra forces concurrency.
