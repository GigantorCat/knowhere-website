The current ratio compares all current assets (cash, receivables, inventory, prepayments) to current liabilities due within twelve months. It includes inventory because even though stock takes time to sell, it represents liquid value. The ideal range of 1.5 to 2 times means for every dollar of short-term debt, the business holds $1.50 to $2.00 in liquid assets. Ratios above 2× suggest capital is sitting idle rather than generating returns through reinvestment or expansion. Ratios below 1× mean current liabilities exceed current assets, creating immediate solvency risk where the business may struggle to meet obligations as they fall due.

Know This
The quick ratio is more stringent than the current ratio because it excludes inventory, which cannot be converted to cash as rapidly as receivables or cash itself.
