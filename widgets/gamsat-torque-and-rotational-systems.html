For a rigid body to stay still — a ladder against a wall, a beam across a gap — you need two conditions, not one. Net force must be zero so it doesn't translate, but net torque must also be zero so it doesn't rotate. A beam balanced on a fulcrum can have forces on both sides: as long as the clockwise torques equal the anticlockwise torques, it stays level. Real-world structures fail when engineers forget the second condition. Bridges twist. Ladders slip. The maths demands both equilibrium equations simultaneously.

Know This

A force can translate an object or rotate it — torque is the measure of turning effect, always calculated as force times perpendicular distance from the pivot, which is why door handles live far from hinges and why equilibrium requires both net force and net torque to be zero.
