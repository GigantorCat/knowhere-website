A risk factor increases your probability of developing disease but doesn't guarantee it. Smoking is a risk factor for lung cancer — it raises your chances by 15–30×, but not every smoker gets cancer and some non-smokers do. Risk factors are probabilistic: they shift population-level odds, not individual certainty. This is why epidemiology studies thousands of people over decades: the pattern only emerges at scale. Changing a risk factor (quitting smoking, improving diet) reduces your probability, but your personal outcome remains uncertain because genetics, chance mutations, and cumulative exposure also play roles.
Know This

Non-infectious diseases dominate modern mortality because risk compounds over time, prevention logic is probabilistic not guaranteed, and socioeconomic gradients shape exposure patterns across populations.
