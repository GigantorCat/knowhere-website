Brighter light doesn't mean more powerful photons

Einstein's photon model revealed that turning up the intensity of light just sends more photons, not stronger ones. Each photon's energy depends only on its frequency. Change the frequency to set each photon's energy, then change the intensity to set how many photons arrive.

Each photon carries energy E = hf, where h is Planck's constant (6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s) and f is frequency. When you increase frequency, each individual photon becomes more energetic following a direct linear relationship. This is why ultraviolet light can damage skin cells while visible light cannot, even if the visible light is much brighter. The frequency determines whether a single photon has enough energy to break molecular bonds. A million low-energy photons hitting simultaneously still cannot do what one high-energy photon can do, because energy transfer happens one photon at a time in quantum interactions.
