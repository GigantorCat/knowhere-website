When oxygen is absent, animal cells convert pyruvate into lactic acid through fermentation. This process doesn't generate any additional ATP beyond the 2 from glycolysis, but it serves a critical function: regenerating NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue running. Without this regeneration step, glycolysis would stall completely, and even the meagre 2 ATP would be unavailable. Lactic acid buildup causes muscle fatigue and the burning sensation during intense exercise, but it's better than zero energy production.

Know This
Aerobic respiration requires oxygen and yields around 30-32 ATP per glucose, while anaerobic respiration yields only 2 ATP from glycolysis alone — but fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue when oxygen is unavailable.
