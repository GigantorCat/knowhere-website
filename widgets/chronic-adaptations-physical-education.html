Aerobic training triggers profound cardiovascular and cellular changes. The heart muscle thickens and chambers enlarge, increasing stroke volume so each beat pumps more blood. New capillaries sprout around muscle fibres, shortening the distance oxygen must diffuse. Inside muscle cells, mitochondria multiply and grow larger, boosting the capacity to generate ATP aerobically. These adaptations explain why trained athletes have lower resting heart rates and can sustain higher workloads — their bodies have literally rebuilt themselves to deliver and use oxygen more efficiently.
Know This
Adaptations are specific to the training done — aerobic work grows mitochondria and capillaries, resistance training thickens muscle fibres, and your body rebuilds itself to match the stress you consistently apply.
