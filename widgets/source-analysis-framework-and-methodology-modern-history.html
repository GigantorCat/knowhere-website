Origin analysis establishes who created the source, when it was produced, and under what circumstances, forming the foundational context for all interpretation. A government memorandum from 1942 carries different weight than a personal diary entry from the same year; a photograph taken by a war correspondent differs fundamentally from one staged for propaganda purposes. Understanding authorship reveals potential access to information, institutional pressures, and the creator's position within power structures. Temporal proximity matters — eyewitness accounts written immediately after events capture visceral detail but lack perspective, while retrospective memoirs gain analytical distance but suffer from memory decay and hindsight bias. The circumstances of creation — whether produced under censorship, created for publication, or intended to remain private — fundamentally shape what information the source contains and how it presents events.
Know This
Historians extract reliable information not by trusting sources, but by systematically interrogating their origin, purpose, and limitations — revealing what even biased or incomplete sources can prove about the past.
