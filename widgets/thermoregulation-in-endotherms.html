When body temperature rises above the hypothalamic set point, thermoreceptors trigger autonomic nervous responses that cause arteriole smooth muscle to relax. This vasodilation dramatically increases blood flow to superficial capillaries in the skin, allowing greater heat dissipation through radiation to the environment. Simultaneously, sweat glands receive sympathetic stimulation to secrete hypotonic fluid onto the skin surface. As this sweat evaporates, it absorbs latent heat of vaporisation directly from the skin, providing immediate and powerful cooling. These rapid responses operate within seconds to minutes, making heat loss highly efficient in endotherms.
Know This
The hypothalamus acts as the body's thermostat, comparing thermoreceptor input against a 37°C set point and coordinating vasomotor, sudomotor, and metabolic responses through both nervous and endocrine pathways.
