Helicoidal flow is a corkscrew current where water doesn't just travel downstream — it spirals across the channel. Maximum velocity sits near the surface on the outside of a bend, slamming the cut bank with enough energy to erode it through hydraulic action and abrasion. Slower water near the bed spirals back toward the inside, dropping its sediment load to build the point bar. That asymmetry is why meanders migrate sideways over time, and why meander wavelength scales so predictably with channel width.

Know This

Helicoidal flow spirals water toward the outer bank, concentrating erosion there while sediment settles on the inner bank — which is why meander wavelength stays reliably 10–14× the channel width.
