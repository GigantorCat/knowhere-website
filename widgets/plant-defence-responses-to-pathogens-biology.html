A resistant paperbark beats myrtle rust by killing its own cells

Myrtle rust (Austropuccinia psidii) reached Australia in 2010 and attacks the soft new leaves of broad-leaved paperbark, Melaleuca quinquenervia. A susceptible tree grows yellow spore pustules within about a fortnight; a resistant tree carries receptors that trigger a ring of deliberate cell death around each spore that breaks in. Advance the days after the spores land and watch where the dead cells appear, then switch genotype and watch what the same spores do in a susceptible leaf.

Know This

In a resistant paperbark the hypersensitive response kills cells only around each myrtle rust entry point, starving the fungus, while systemic acquired resistance primes the rest of the leaf without sterilising it.
