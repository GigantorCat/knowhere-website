Plants deploy physical barriers as a first line of defence. The waxy cuticle coating leaf surfaces repels water and blocks pathogen entry points. Cell walls provide structural resistance, while bark creates a thick protective layer on stems. When threatened, stomata — the pores through which plants breathe — can close within minutes, sealing potential invasion routes. These passive barriers work constantly, requiring no energy investment beyond their initial construction, making them the most economical defence strategy in the plant's arsenal.
Know This
The hypersensitive response sacrifices infected cells to create a dead zone that starves the pathogen, while systemic acquired resistance puts the entire plant on alert, priming distant tissues to resist future attacks.
