The oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve: a live curve showing how much oxygen haemoglobin loads in the lungs and hands to tissue, and how CO₂, pH, temperature and 2,3-BPG shift it

Haemoglobin’s real job is letting go.

Blood leaves the lungs about 97% saturated with oxygen, yet resting tissue takes only about a quarter of it. The S-shape of the oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve, and the way it shifts, decide how much is handed over. Lower the tissue PO₂, then raise CO₂, acid, heat or 2,3-BPG and watch how much oxygen haemoglobin lets go.

Know this

Haemoglobin’s cooperative binding makes the curve S-shaped, and more CO₂, acid, heat or 2,3-BPG causes a right shift: a higher P50, so working tissue is handed more oxygen.
