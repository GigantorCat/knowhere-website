Index laws and surds: an index ladder, an index line for the three laws, and a solver that matches bases

A square root is just a power of one half

Every surd is a power in disguise: √2 is 2 to the power of ½, and ⅛ is 2 to the power of −3. One small set of index laws runs whole numbers, zero, negatives, fractions and surds alike. Change the index and watch each step up the ladder multiply by the base.

Know this

Multiplying powers of one base is adding the indices, so a negative index means a reciprocal and a fractional index means a root: 2−1 = ½ and 21/2 = √2.
