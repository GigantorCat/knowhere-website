A whole new animal can start from one nucleus

Reproductive cloning makes a whole organism that is genetically identical to another. Embryo splitting divides one early embryo into twins; somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT), the method behind Dolly the sheep, puts a body cell’s nucleus into an emptied egg. Therapeutic cloning uses somatic cell nuclear transfer (SCNT) to create embryonic stem cells for research and potential treatments — the embryo is never implanted, so no organism develops. Reproductive cloning (like Dolly) uses the same SCNT technique but implants the embryo to produce a live animal. The biology is identical; the ethical dividing line is whether you let development continue. Dolly proved mammals could be cloned from adult cells, but success rates remain below 5% — most embryos fail, and survivors often have health defects. Meanwhile, natural clones (identical twins, asexual organisms like strawberry runners) happen all the time with zero intervention and 100% viability.
Know This

Reproductive cloning (embryo splitting or SCNT) makes a whole organism with the same nuclear DNA as its twin or its donor; SCNT is still inefficient and raises welfare concerns. Gene cloning copies one gene in bacteria, not an organism, and natural clones such as identical twins arise without any technology.
