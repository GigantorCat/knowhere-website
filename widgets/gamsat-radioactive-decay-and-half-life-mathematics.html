Alpha decay occurs when a nucleus emits a helium-4 nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons), decreasing the mass number A by 4 and atomic number Z by 2. For example, uranium-238 decays to thorium-234 plus an alpha particle. The ejected alpha particle carries significant kinetic energy but has low penetration — stopped by paper or skin. This decay mode is common in heavy nuclei (Z > 82) where the strong nuclear force struggles to contain excess nucleons. Alpha emitters have half-lives from microseconds to billions of years, depending on the isotope.
Know This
In each half-life, about half of the nuclei still left decay, whatever the starting amount. After n half-lives a fraction (½)n remains, and the activity halves every half-life too. For times between whole half-lives the same curve is N = N0e−λt, with λ = ln 2 ÷ t½ and activity A = λN.
