At the same depth, a thin tube of water presses as hard as the ocean

Pressure in a still fluid comes from the weight of fluid stacked above a point, so it depends on how deep the point is and how dense the fluid is, not on how much fluid there is or what shape holds it. It pushes in every direction at once. Lower the probe, switch to seawater or mercury, and watch the pressure arrows in all three vessels and the line on the graph.

Know this

Pressure in a still fluid is P₀ + ρgh: it grows with depth and density, pushes equally in every direction, and ignores the shape of the container.
