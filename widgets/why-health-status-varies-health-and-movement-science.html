The exam tests whether you can classify factors correctly. Biological factors are body-based: body weight, blood pressure, birth weight, glucose regulation, immune response, genetics. Sociocultural factors are about people and society: income, education level, employment status, culture, religion, social support networks, family structure. Environmental factors are about surroundings: housing quality, workplace conditions, infrastructure access, geographic location (remote vs urban), exposure to pollution. All three operate together to explain why some groups have higher rates of disease, disability and premature death than others.

Know This

Health status variations between Australian population groups result from the interaction of biological (body weight, blood pressure, genetics), sociocultural (income, education, culture, social support), and environmental (housing, work, geographic location) factors — exam questions require correct classification.
