These four layers operate in sequence but overlap in time. Barriers slow entry immediately — skin as a dry desert, mucus as a sticky trap, cilia sweeping upward, stomach acid at pH 2 dissolving proteins. The moment a pathogen breaches, inflammation redirects blood flow to the site: vessels dilate, plasma leaks, the tissue swells and reddens as reinforcements flood in. Fever follows as the hypothalamus resets the body's thermostat higher, speeding immune reactions while slowing pathogen replication. Phagocytes — neutrophils and macrophages — arrive and engulf invaders whole, digesting them in acidic vacuoles. Each defence is a physical or chemical change in host tissue, not a failure but a coordinated escalation.
Know This

Inflammation and fever are not symptoms of damage but deliberate physical changes that create battlefield advantage before specialised immunity ever arrives.
