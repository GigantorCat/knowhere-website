Pasteur's swan-neck flask let air in but trapped dust and microbes in the curve. Broth stayed clear for months. Break the neck, and within days the broth teemed with life. Spontaneous generation was dead — microbes came from microbes, not thin air. This simple experiment forced medicine to look for living causes of disease, not bad vapours or moral failings. Pasteurisation applied the same insight: heat kills microbes, careful handling keeps them out.
