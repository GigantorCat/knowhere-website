One cell, two divisions, four different gametes

Meiosis copies the DNA once, then divides twice, so one diploid cell becomes four haploid gametes. On the way it shuffles the alleles: partner chromosomes swap pieces, and the pairs line up by chance. Step through the phases and count how many different gametes come out.
