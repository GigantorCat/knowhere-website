Resting membrane potential: a slice of neuron membrane with potassium and sodium leak channels and the sodium-potassium pump, beside a voltage scale showing where the membrane potential settles

The pump builds the gradient. The −70 mV comes from a leak.

Inside a resting neuron, potassium is about 28 times more concentrated than outside, and the membrane is dotted with channels that let it leak out. Every K⁺ that escapes leaves a negative charge behind, until the inside is negative enough to hold the rest back. Raise the outside potassium or the sodium leak, stop the pump, and watch where the membrane potential settles.

Know this

A resting neuron sits near −70 mV because its membrane leaks mostly K⁺, which holds it close to EK (about −90 mV), while a small Na⁺ leak pulls it up; the pump only keeps the gradients in place.
