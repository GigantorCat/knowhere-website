Testing a formula on a handful of values is not a proof: the next value could still break it. Induction verifies n = 1 once, then takes an unspecified k, assumes the formula there, and shows the next term carries it to k + 1. Switch to the inductive step and watch the term k + 1 join the assumed sum; then change k and notice the algebra never changes.

Know This

The inductive step is one argument about an arbitrary k: write the sum to k + 1 as the assumed sum plus the next term, then factorise to the formula with k + 1 in place of k.
