Harder practice now means easier retention

Random practice feels messier than blocked drills—and looks worse during practice—but it usually leads to better retention once the practice stops. Beginners often start with blocked practice to get the basic movement, then shift toward random practice as they improve. The same paradox applies to feedback: intrinsic (what you felt) often teaches more than constant external correction. Intrinsic feedback is what the learner feels directly—the weight of the ball leaving your hand, the resistance in the water, the timing of your landing. It's always available during performance and forces the athlete to build internal references. Early-stage learners often need augmented feedback (coach cues, video analysis, metrics) to know what "good" feels like, but over-reliance delays the shift to self-monitoring. Skilled performers use intrinsic signals to self-correct in real time; they've internalised the feel of the movement and can adjust without external input.
Know This
Random practice creates desirable difficulty: retrieval and reconstruction strengthen memory more than repetition, and intrinsic feedback builds autonomy faster than constant correction.
