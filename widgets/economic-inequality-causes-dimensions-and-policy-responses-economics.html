How much of a wealth gap comes from inheritance?

Drag the slider to change A’s inheritance and see how much of the wealth gap at 65 comes from pay and how much from inheritance in this example. In real data the answer is contested: the Productivity Commission (2021) found inheritances add to dollar gaps but slightly reduce some measures of relative wealth inequality in Australia.

The gender pay gap measures the difference between average male and female earnings, typically expressed as a percentage of male earnings. Part of the raw gap is explained by differences in occupation and industry (occupational segregation into lower-paid sectors), hours worked and career breaks for unpaid care. The part left after controlling for those factors points to discrimination and other factors that are hard to measure. The gap widens after childbirth and compounds over a lifetime, directly feeding into retirement wealth inequality. Measurement matters: full-time equivalent vs actual earnings, mean vs median, and whether superannuation is included all shift the headline figure.

Inequality has three causes — market forces, institutions, and inheritance — and policy can target each with a distinct tool.
