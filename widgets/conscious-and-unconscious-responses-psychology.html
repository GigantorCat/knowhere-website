An unconscious response (reflex arc) routes directly through the spinal cord without cortical involvement. When you touch something hot, sensory neurons fire to the spinal cord, which immediately activates motor neurons to withdraw your hand — all in 50–80 milliseconds. Your brain learns about the burn after your hand has already moved. This speed advantage is pure survival: the cortex is slow because it deliberates, weighs options, considers context. The reflex arc doesn't think. It just acts. Both pathways use the same neurons, the same action potentials, the same neurotransmitters — the only difference is whether the signal makes a detour through your frontal lobe.

Know This

Conscious responses are voluntary and deliberate but slower because they route through the brain for decision-making, while unconscious responses (reflexes) bypass the brain entirely and fire at the spinal cord — the speed difference is the survival advantage.
