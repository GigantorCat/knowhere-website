Access to safe water and sanitation remains the foundation of health — yet 2.2 billion people globally lack safe drinking water, and 4.2 billion lack safely managed sanitation. Waterborne diseases kill nearly 1.4 million people annually, most of them children under five. The burden falls overwhelmingly on low-income countries, where unsafe water drives diarrheal disease, cholera, typhoid, and parasitic infections. When communities lack basic infrastructure, every health intervention downstream becomes harder — maternal mortality rises, malnutrition worsens, chronic disease accelerates. Safe water isn't just one factor among many; it's the precondition for almost every other health gain.
Know This
Access to safe water and sanitation, poverty, inequality and discrimination, and the global marketing of tobacco, alcohol, and processed food drive differences in health status — not randomly, but systematically concentrating disease burden in the world's poorest populations.
