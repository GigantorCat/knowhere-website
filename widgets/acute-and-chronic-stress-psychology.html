Acute stress is sudden, intense, and short-lived — your body's adaptive response to immediate threat. When you encounter danger, adrenaline floods your system within seconds: heart rate spikes, pupils dilate, blood rushes to muscles, and glucose floods your bloodstream. This is the fight-or-flight response, refined over millions of years to give you maximum performance in the moment. The system is designed to switch on hard, then switch off completely once the threat passes. Your immune system actually gets a temporary boost during acute stress, preparing to fight potential infections from injuries. This is your body working exactly as evolved — a brief, intense mobilization that saves your life.
Know This
Acute stress is adrenaline-driven and adaptive; chronic stress is cortisol-driven and wears down your immune system over time — the same response mechanism that saves you in minutes damages you when sustained for months.
