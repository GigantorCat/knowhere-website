Black body curves and Wien's law: changing the temperature redraws the emission curve of a glowing object against wavelength, next to the Sun's curve, showing the peak moving to shorter wavelengths and the area under the curve growing as the object gets hotter.

Heat a star and it gives off more red light, not less. Its peak just slides to blue

Every warm object glows across a whole spread of wavelengths, and the shape of that spread is set by its temperature alone. Hotter objects emit more at every wavelength, peak at a shorter wavelength and pour out far more energy in total. Raise the temperature and watch the curve grow past the Sun's.
