Forces That Push Back

Gravity only attracts — mass always pulls on mass. But electric forces can repel. The difference? Charge comes in two signs, and like charges push each other away with exactly the same inverse-square law that unlike charges use to attract. Like charges repel with the same inverse-square law that unlike charges use to attract. When you place two positive charges near each other, the force pushes them apart — field lines emanate from both, never crossing, creating a pressure between them. The mathematics is identical to attraction, but the sign of the force flips. This is the fundamental difference from gravity: mass only attracts, but charge can push. Field lines pointing outward from positive charges (or inward to negative) map the direction a positive test charge would accelerate.
Know This

Coulomb's law is an inverse-square law identical in structure to Newton's gravity, but charge comes in two signs — like charges repel, unlike attract — giving electric forces a push-and-pull versatility that gravity lacks.
