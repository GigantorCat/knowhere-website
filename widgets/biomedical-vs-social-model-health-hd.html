Biomedical strengths: Rapid response to acute illness, advanced surgical interventions, precise diagnosis through technology, evidence-based pharmaceutical treatments. Social strengths: Addresses root causes like poverty and education, prevents disease before onset, cost-effective at population scale, reduces health inequalities by tackling determinants. Together they form complementary approaches — biomedical saves lives in crisis; social prevents crises from occurring.
Know This
The biomedical model focuses on diagnosis and treatment of illness after it occurs, while the social model addresses the broader determinants that prevent disease — one is reactive; the other is preventive, and each has distinct strengths and limitations depending on the health challenge.
