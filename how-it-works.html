MODEL 01

Style → why you learn

Nine learner styles, mapped from how motivation actually works — not "are you a visual learner" quiz-fluff. Most "personalised learning" is a recommendation engine deciding your next video; this is the content itself, rebuilt for your psychology — so you start in psychological safety, in your best learning state. Your style decides the hook (what pulls you in), the framing (the angle that makes it make sense), and the study moves (how you should attack it). Same concept, nine genuinely different ways in.

The Perfectionist

Wants it right. Gets clear criteria, worked precision, and the why behind every rule.

The Helper

Learns through people. Gets human stakes, real-world impact, study-with-others moves.

The Achiever

Plays to win. Gets exam leverage, measurable targets, and the fastest route to marks.

The Individualist

Needs meaning. Gets the story, the elegance, the reason this idea matters at all.

The Investigator

Goes deep. Gets first principles, the mechanism underneath, permission to rabbit-hole.

The Loyalist

Trusts the tested path. Gets structure, checkpoints, and proof the method works.

The Generalist

Runs on novelty. Gets plot twists, connections everywhere, four rabbit holes per concept.

The Challenger

Learns by wrestling. Gets bold claims to test, control of the pace, no hand-holding.

The Peacemaker

Settles in gently. Gets calm sequencing, low pressure, and momentum built quietly.

MODEL 02

Neurology → how you process

ADHD, ASD, dyslexia — these aren't deficits to work around. They're operating systems, each with genuine strengths and real friction points. The neurology layer doesn't water anything down; it reshapes the format so the friction disappears and the strengths do the lifting.

Neurology layer

Same knowledge. Rebuilt delivery.

When the neurology layer is on, content is restructured end to end: how it opens, how strategies are framed, how quizzes are formatted, even how encouragement is worded. Nothing is removed. Everything is re-cut.

ADHD

Interest-first hooks, short sprints with real breaks, one question at a time, instant feedback, zero guilt mechanics.

ASD

Rules stated before application, explicit marking criteria, precise wording, no ambiguity, no format surprises mid-task.

Dyslexia

Minimal text, visuals that carry the meaning, audio one tap away, explain-it-back-out-loud over written notes.

Knowhere was designed by a neurodivergent founder — for neurodivergent students and typical neurology alike. Not as an accessibility checkbox bolted on at the end, but as the starting assumption: brains are different, so the format should be too. Whatever your neurology, you get an equal playing field to learn on. That's the whole point.

THE EQUALISER

300+ concepts carrying content other states examine. you get their angles anyway, clearly badged.

Somewhere right now, a student in another state is being taught something you’d never even be shown. Not anymore: if it’s examinable anywhere in Australia, it’s in your library. The curriculum decides what’s examinable — not what you’re allowed to understand. Every extra carries a clear badge — “not examinable in your state” — so exam prep stays pure and curiosity gets fed. Nobody else does this, because nobody else maps every state into one library.
